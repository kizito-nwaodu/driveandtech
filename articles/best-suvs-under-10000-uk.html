10 Best SUVs Under £10,000 UK

A DriveAndTech comparison of used SUVs for buyers who care about reliability, running costs, practicality, comfort, safety and long-term value.

With a £10,000 budget, the used SUV market offers strong choices, but the cheapest vehicle is not necessarily the smartest purchase. Condition, service history, running costs and known ownership issues can matter more than age or specification.

Quick answer

Best overall: Honda CR-V. DriveAndTech's top recommendation combines strong reliability, a spacious cabin, useful boot space, comfortable motorway manners and sensible ownership costs. The Toyota RAV4 is the reliability pick, while the Kia Sportage is the family choice.

DriveAndTech buying principle: recommendations are assessed using reliability, typical running costs, fuel economy, safety, practicality, comfort, parts availability, resale value and overall ownership experience.

1. Honda CR-V

The CR-V takes the overall award because it avoids major weaknesses. It offers a spacious cabin, generous rear room, a large boot and comfortable long-distance driving. DriveAndTech's recommended model years are 2013–2016, with the 2.0 i-VTEC petrol manual as the preferred engine for most buyers. A 1.6 i-DTEC diesel can make sense for regular motorway mileage.

What to check

  • Full service history and MOT history.
  • Clutch operation on manual examples.
  • Automatic gearbox servicing where applicable.
  • Suspension noises and uneven tyre wear.
  • Air conditioning, parking sensors and reversing camera.

2. Toyota RAV4

The RAV4 is the reliability winner. DriveAndTech recommends 2012–2015 examples, with the 2.0 D-4D diesel manual suited to longer-distance drivers and the 2.0 VVT-i petrol worth considering for shorter urban journeys. Buyers should pay particular attention to service records, suspension, gearbox operation, DPF condition on diesels and the four-wheel-drive system where fitted.

3. Kia Sportage

The Sportage earns the family award. Recommended 2014–2017 examples offer useful equipment, attractive styling, good passenger space and a large boot. The 1.7 CRDi diesel is preferred for regular motorway use, while the 1.6 GDi petrol is more suitable for frequent short urban trips.

4. Hyundai Tucson

The Tucson is the value pick. DriveAndTech recommends 2015–2018 examples and highlights its combination of newer styling, generous equipment and sensible ownership costs. The 1.7 CRDi is the preferred engine for longer journeys, while the 1.6 GDi petrol avoids diesel particulate filter concerns associated with frequent short trips.

5–10: Strong alternatives

Nissan Qashqai stands out for ownership costs. Mazda CX-5 is the driver's choice. Ford Kuga is the comfort option. Volvo XC60 brings a premium feel. Skoda Yeti prioritises practicality. Subaru Forester is the adventure-oriented choice.

What makes a great used SUV?

Look for proven reliability, affordable servicing, good fuel economy, strong safety credentials, comfortable everyday driving, practical passenger and boot space, available replacement parts and reasonable resale value. A well-maintained SUV with a full service history can be a better purchase than a newer vehicle with poor maintenance records.

Before you buy

Check the vehicle's history, condition, MOT record, service documentation, tyres, suspension, brakes, gearbox, air conditioning, infotainment and electrical equipment. Diesel SUVs deserve particular attention if the previous owner mainly made short journeys.

DriveAndTech verdict

There is no single SUV that is perfect for every buyer. For the broadest combination of reliability, practicality and ownership confidence, DriveAndTech places the Honda CR-V first. The Toyota RAV4 is the stronger choice when reliability is the overriding priority, while the Sportage and Tucson make strong family and value propositions.
